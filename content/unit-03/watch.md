# Watch out

Notes where your study guide, cheat sheets, slides and textbook disagree or are easy to mix up. Each starts with the deck it belongs to.

- (deck 3) Safavid–Mughal rivalry: your study guide and the 3.1 slides say religion (Shi'a Safavids vs. Sunni Mughals) sharpened it. AMSCO 3.4 says religion mattered less there than in the Ottoman–Safavid war; the core was control of resources and trade routes in present-day Afghanistan (Kandahar). Safe answer: Ottoman–Safavid = religion and territory; Safavid–Mughal = mainly territory and trade, with some religious tension.
- (deck 2) Which states are "gunpowder empires": your study guide groups three (Ottoman, Safavid, Mughal). The Pocket Dictionary and the 3.1 organizer add the Qing. AMSCO counts the three Islamic empires plus Russia. All of them used firearms to expand; if a question names "the gunpowder empires" without a list, it usually means the three Islamic ones.
- (deck 2) The Qing ended in 1911 (AMSCO: the revolution) or 1912 (your cheat sheets: the last emperor stepped down). Both are right about different events.
- (deck 3) Vienna was besieged twice. 1529 (Suleiman) = the farthest Ottoman push. 1683 = the failure that marked the turning point of Ottoman decline in Europe.
- (deck 4) Zamindars: your slides call them local landholding elites who collected taxes; AMSCO calls them paid officials under Akbar who later got land grants and, under later emperors, kept more of the taxes and built private armies. Both agree on the key point: they collected taxes and kept a share.
- (deck 4) Tax farming vs. zamindars: both let someone other than the central government collect taxes and keep part. Tax farming = Ottoman, collectors pay a fixed sum up front. Zamindars = Mughal, local collectors keep a share.
- (deck 4) Ghulams (Safavid) and Janissaries (Ottoman) are parallel: both enslaved, both from minority groups, both loyal only to the ruler. Don't mix up which empire each belongs to.
- (deck 4) Divine right (Europe) and the Mandate of Heaven (China) both tie rule to the divine, but the Mandate can be lost through bad rule, disaster or revolt.
- (deck 4) Your study guide mentions the Mughal "mansabdar system" in a table but no source explains it. Don't rely on it; use zamindars for Mughal administration.
- (deck 2) Same names, different spellings: Zunghars = Dzungars; Cuzco = Cusco; AMSCO prints the Qing emperor as "Quinlong" in one quote; it is Qianlong.
- (deck 2) Your class's practice handout spells it "Songhay" and "Safavi"; the kit uses Songhai and Safavid. Same empires.
- (deck 2) Unit 3 is about LAND-based empires. Sea empires (Portugal, Spain in the Americas) are Unit 4. Your 3.1 organizer says Europeans built ocean empires "in the same years."
- (deck 5) Peace of Augsburg (1555): ruler chooses Catholic or Lutheran. Peace of Westphalia (1648): Calvinism added as a third choice.
- (deck 5) Luther = indulgences, faith alone (1517). Calvin = predestination, Geneva, Huguenots and Puritans. Henry VIII = Anglican Church, over an annulment.
