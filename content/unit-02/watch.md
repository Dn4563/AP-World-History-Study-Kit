# Watch out

Notes where the slides, review sheet or textbook disagree or are easy to misread. Each starts with the deck it belongs to.

- (deck 4) The Fiveable cheat sheet lists salt among the goods from sub-Saharan Africa. That is backwards: salt came from the north (for example Taghaza) and moved south; gold moved north. The textbook and review doc agree. Also, the textbook is inconsistent about cloth (its text sends cloth south, its table sends cloth north), so do not rely on cloth's direction.
- (deck 2) The textbook says Hulegu converted to Islam and places the Il-khanate in "Central Asia." The safe version for the test: the Mongols of the Il-khanate (in Persia and Southwest Asia) later converted to Islam.
- (deck 2) The textbook says Kublai defeated the Song in 1271. The Yuan was founded in 1271, but the Song itself ended in 1279 (the textbook dates the Song as 960–1279 elsewhere).
- (deck 2) The textbook says some consider the cannon a Mongol invention and that Mongol tactics ended knights in armor and walled cities. These are overstatements. The safe test line, matching the cheat sheets: the Mongols spread gunpowder and siege technology; they did not invent it.
- (deck 2) The textbook says the Mamluks and Crusaders formed a temporary alliance to defeat Hulegu in 1260. The Crusader role is usually described as limited. Safe version: Mamluks under Baibars halted the Mongols in 1260.
- (deck 2) The textbook says the Golden Horde lost its territory by about 1369, yet also describes Kulikovo in 1380 and Mongol influence in Russia lasting longer. Safe version: Kulikovo (1380) began the Golden Horde's decline, and Moscow rose.
- (deck 2) The textbook says the Mongols brought the "Arabic numbering system" to Europe. The numerals began in India; use "Hindu-Arabic."
- (deck 3) The textbook says Zheng He reached the Cape of Good Hope, but its own map shows East Africa. Say East Africa. Also say "Ming admiral" (the textbook calls him a Muslim admiral).
- (deck 3) Monsoon wording differs: the textbook says winter winds come from the northeast and summer winds from the southwest; the review doc says summer winds blow southwest to northeast. Same pattern, described by where the wind comes from versus where it goes. Know both.
- (deck 5) The textbook calls Srivijaya a Hindu kingdom and Majapahit Buddhist. Historians generally describe Srivijaya as a major Buddhist center and Majapahit as Hindu-Buddhist. Safest label on the test: Hindu-Buddhist kingdoms.
- (deck 1) The textbook says caravanserai were about 100 miles apart. Spacing varied; what matters is that they were spaced so travelers and animals could rest and resupply. Also, the textbook's table dates (banking house c. 200 B.C.E., bill of exchange c. 700 C.E., Hanseatic League 1296) conflict with its own text. Just know these practices predate 1200 and expanded afterward.
- (deck 4) The textbook says Mansa Musa traveled with 100 camels; other accounts describe a much larger caravan. Do not memorize a number. Know: lavish gold, 1324, Mecca, then mosques and schools in Timbuktu.
- (deck 6) The textbook says South Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa were spared the plague because they had few ports. That claim is contested; do not rely on it. Also the plague's origin is described differently (Central Asia, southern China, East Asia); safe version: it began in Asia and spread along trade routes. Death tolls vary by source; "about a third of Europe" is the safest figure.
- (deck 6) The review doc says Champa rice ripened in about 60 days and gave two to three harvests; the textbook says quick-ripening with two crops a year, and some scholars think it originated in India. Safe version: quick-ripening, two crops a year, from Champa (Vietnam).
- (deck 5) The review doc lists Kashgar only as a growing city. The textbook adds that it later declined after Tamerlane (1389–90). Both are true: it rose with the Silk Roads and declined with invasion.
- (deck 7) The textbook introduces the Vijayanagara reservoir quote as from the 1300s, but the source (Domingo Paes) is dated 1520–22. Use it as an example of large labor projects, not as 1200–1450 evidence.
- (deck 5) The textbook says Islam spread through merchants, missionaries, and conquests generally. For West Africa, the Swahili coast, and Malacca, the safe answer is merchants and rulers' conversion, not conquest.
